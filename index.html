About Me

I don’t believe grief needs to be fixed or rushed. I believe it’s love continuing — asking to be acknowledged. My role isn’t to push you forward or tell you how to feel. It’s to walk beside you in the in-between: before goodbye, after loss, and in the moments where love still feels present. I listen carefully — not just to what’s said, but to what’s felt. Every grief journey is different.

This work is personal. I found my way here through the loss of my dog, Piggy — my protector, my shadow, and my soulmate. He was with me through some of the hardest years of my life, offering steady, unconditional love when I needed it most. When Piggy was diagnosed with oral melanoma, I devoted every moment I could to caring for him.

We were given ten more months together before I helped him cross into a place without pain or suffering. It’s been years, and I still miss him deeply. That love didn’t disappear — it changed. Losing Piggy was the most painful loss I’ve experienced. And it’s also what showed me how profound, enduring, and real this kind of love truly is.

My work is rooted in both professional training and deeply personal experience. I bring a steady, compassionate presence to those navigating anticipatory grief, loss, and the enduring bond we share with the animals we love.

I hold a Master’s degree in Counseling and a Bachelor’s degree in Psychology from California University of Pennsylvania, where I developed a strong foundation in emotional support, active listening, and human-centered care.

I further expanded my work through formal life coach training with Coach Training Alliance, strengthening my ability to hold space with intention, clarity, and respect for each individual’s unique process.

Beyond formal education, this work has been shaped by my own lived experience of loving and losing a deeply bonded companion. That experience continues to inform the way I show up — gently, without rushing, and without trying to fix what is not meant to be fixed.
